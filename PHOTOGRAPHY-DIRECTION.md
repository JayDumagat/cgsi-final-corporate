# CGSI institutional photography direction

This brief defines how photography should make Caballes-Go Securities, Inc. feel human,
institutional, locally grounded, and precise. It applies to commissioned shoots, licensed stock,
CMS uploads, public pages, publications, social previews, and future offering pages.

## 1. Creative proposition

**Market discipline, human accountability, Philippine context.**

CGSI photography should show the real environments and relationships around investing: listening,
reviewing, explaining, deciding, executing, and following through. The work should feel observed
rather than staged.

The desired emotional balance is:

- assured, not theatrical;
- premium, not luxurious for its own sake;
- contemporary, not speculative fintech;
- optimistic, not euphoric;
- personal, not informal;
- locally recognizable, not stereotyped;
- precise, not sterile.

Photography should support a section’s meaning. It is not a requirement for every card or every
paragraph.

## 2. Philippine visual context

The library should gradually build a distinct sense of place through truthful details:

- Ortigas or another verified CGSI business context;
- Philippine business districts, streets, architecture, weather, and natural light;
- Filipino professionals in credible offices and meeting environments;
- multigenerational Filipino families discussing real decisions;
- OFWs and seafarers shown with dignity, skill, and agency;
- young professionals and graduates beginning to manage their finances;
- local-market work such as research review, order coordination, settlement preparation, and
  client communication.

Do not use a location, office, trading floor, regulator building, exchange facility, ship, or
person in a way that implies a CGSI relationship without permission and factual review.

Philippine identity should emerge from the environment, people, climate, clothing, and activity.
It should not depend on flags, tourist landmarks, jeepneys, or other shorthand unless the subject
genuinely calls for them.

## 3. Image-making style

### People and direction

- Prefer real professionals, clients with written consent, or carefully cast Filipino talent.
- Direct people into a credible task or conversation instead of asking them to pose at the camera.
- Capture listening, pauses, note-taking, explanation, concentration, and natural interaction.
- Allow normal human detail: used notebooks, a jacket on a chair, changing daylight, imperfect
  posture, and lived-in but orderly rooms.
- Smiles should arise from the interaction. Not every subject needs to smile.
- Represent the site’s 23-to-60-plus audience, families, new professionals, OFWs, seafarers,
  institutional users, and people with disabilities without token placement.
- Wardrobe should suit the Philippine climate and the person’s role: polished business or smart
  business-casual clothing, not compulsory dark suits.

### Light and color

- Use available or natural-looking light with controlled contrast.
- Keep skin tones accurate and retain texture.
- Favor neutral whites, deep blues, warm wood, concrete, glass, and restrained greens already
  present in real environments.
- Do not force the brand green into props, lighting, wardrobe, or every image.
- Avoid teal-and-orange grading, heavy HDR, crushed shadows, fluorescent green tint, and
  oversaturated city lights.
- Keep a consistent neutral-to-slightly-warm grade across the library.

### Composition

- Build depth with foreground, subject, and environment instead of placing people against empty
  studio walls.
- Capture horizontal and vertical alternatives when commissioning a shoot.
- Leave deliberate copy space only when the planned page composition requires it.
- Place important faces, hands, and work material away from likely responsive crop boundaries.
- Use asymmetry, interrupted sight lines, and off-center subjects when they improve the editorial
  composition.
- Photograph details and environments alongside portraits; a credible institutional library
  cannot consist only of faces.

### Technical baseline

- Retain a high-resolution, uncropped master, preferably at least 3,000 pixels on the long edge.
- Deliver in sRGB with no baked-in text, gradients, logos, or interface overlays.
- Capture a 3:2 or 4:3 master plus approved 16:9, 4:5, and 1:1 derivatives when needed.
- Preserve metadata needed for rights and source tracking.
- Optimize web derivatives through the approved image pipeline; do not repeatedly recompress the
  original.

## 4. Priority shot list

| Priority | Scene | What it communicates | Framing and page use |

| 1 | Broker or advisor listening to a client and making notes | Personal ownership and attention | Wide environmental frame with copy space; homepage or broker-assisted trading |
| 2 | Two professionals reviewing research together | Judgment, challenge, and collaboration | Over-shoulder and medium variants; research service and insights |
| 3 | Operations staff reviewing a sanitized settlement checklist | Follow-through after execution | Hands, documents, and environment; settlement and custody |
| 4 | Advisor explaining a concept to a couple or family | Clarity around shared decisions | Natural seated interaction; individuals and families |

| 6 | Seafarer or land-based OFW in a real work or communication context | Continuity across distance | Environment-led portrait plus remote-call detail; OFW and seafarer page |
| 7 | Young Filipino professional reviewing a first investment plan | A considered beginning | Desk or workplace scene, not a phone celebration; new-investor page |
| 8 | Institutional client meeting with a small, credible team | Professional process and local-market capability | Wide meeting-room composition; institutional client page |
| 9 | Professional handling a client call in an actual operations environment | Responsiveness and accountability | Side profile, layered work setting; service overview |

For commissioned work, obtain wide, medium, and detail coverage of each core scenario. A coherent
story is more valuable than many unrelated stock photographs.

## 5. Page-level use

- Use one dominant image to carry a major section. Do not add a small image to every service card.
- A homepage should normally use one defining hero image and two to four meaningful editorial
  images below it.
- Avoid placing two unrelated people photographs directly beside each other.
- Reuse a signature image only when it represents the same subject; do not let one stock photo
  stand for several different audiences.
- Give publications their own image language: real authors, locations, objects, economic subjects,
  or approved abstract editorial work rather than generic advisor meetings.
- Governance pages may use architecture, process, people at work, and documentary details. Avoid
  gavels, padlocks, shields, stamps, and staged handshakes.
- Product and platform pages should show the actual approved product experience or user context.
  Do not fabricate interfaces inside a photograph.
- Keep text overlays short and ensure the image remains understandable without the text.

## 6. Anti-generic-stock criteria

Reject an image if one or more of these is central to it:

- a handshake used as the entire idea;
- coins stacked beside a seedling;
- glowing charts reflected in glasses;
- a finger touching a floating graph;
- fake dashboards, holograms, digital globes, or cryptocurrency imagery;
- models pointing at an empty laptop while smiling at the camera;
- a boardroom filled with interchangeable, perfectly styled people;
- exaggerated celebration, fist pumps, or market euphoria;
- a worried senior holding bills or a calculator;
- private jets, yachts, watches, sports cars, and other shorthand for high net worth;
- U.S. dollars, foreign ticker symbols, or a non-Philippine exchange presented as local context;
- immaculate offices with no trace of real work;
- aggressive green lighting or props chosen only to match the logo;
- an image that could advertise a hospital, insurer, software company, or property developer
  without changing anything;
- visible client names, account values, signatures, IDs, order details, contact information, or
  other confidential data;
- synthetic or AI-generated people presented as documentary CGSI photography.

An image is a strong candidate when:

1. the activity could plausibly happen in the described CGSI or client context;
2. the subject has a reason to be in the frame;
3. the environment provides useful Philippine or professional context;
4. body language feels observed rather than performed;
5. the crop has a clear editorial purpose;
6. it adds information or emotion that the copy cannot provide alone.

## 7. Sourcing hierarchy

Use this order of preference:

1. **Commissioned Philippine photography** with a written brief, usage agreement, releases, and
   sanitized environments.
2. **Existing CGSI-owned photography** after rights, consent, quality, and confidentiality review.
3. **Reputable licensed stock** that meets this brief and has sufficient model/property-release
   documentation.
4. **Rights-cleared archival or editorial photography** only when its license permits the exact
   use and no endorsement is implied.

Do not download imagery from search results, social media, news sites, employee profiles, partner
sites, or regulator/exchange pages without explicit rights.

Pexels and similar libraries may permit broad commercial use, but the team must still review the
current license, prohibited uses, recognizable people, sensitive context, trademarks, property
rights, and implied endorsement for each asset. A free download is not the same as an unrestricted
financial-services endorsement.

Official license references should be checked at the time of download:

- Pexels license: <https://www.pexels.com/license/>
- Unsplash license: <https://unsplash.com/license>

## 8. Commissioning, releases, and consent

A commissioned-photography agreement should define:

- worldwide digital, web, social, publication, recruitment, and presentation use;
- duration, exclusivity, territory, paid-media rights, sublicensing, and archive rights;
- the right to crop, resize, color-correct, caption, and create accessibility derivatives;
- ownership or licensed use of raw files and final selects;
- model releases for every recognizable person;
- parent or guardian release for minors;
- location and property releases where required;
- photographer credit requirements;
- restrictions, expiration, withdrawal, and takedown process.

Clients must never be photographed or identified for marketing merely because they are present.
Obtain separate, informed consent that does not affect their service relationship. Employees and
talent should understand where and for how long their image may appear.

## 9. Privacy and operational safety

- Use sanitized documents and staged, nonfunctional account information.
- Remove names, account numbers, signatures, QR codes, ID documents, addresses, phone numbers,
  emails, calendars, passwords, badges, whiteboards, and screen notifications.
- Confirm that reflections in glass, monitors, glasses, and polished surfaces reveal no sensitive
  information.
- Do not photograph live order tickets, active client conversations, restricted research, access
  credentials, security controls, or office access patterns.
- Review uniforms, vessel names, employer marks, building signage, and third-party logos before
  publication.
- Compliance, the Data Protection Officer, information security, and the relevant operations
  owner should review scenes that touch regulated or personal information.

## 10. Licensing and asset records

Every public image needs an entry in the approved media register and, where repository assets are
used, in `IMAGE-SOURCES.md`.

Record:

- internal asset ID and final filename;
- title or scene description;
- photographer and agency or source;
- original source URL;
- download or shoot date;
- exact license or contract version;
- permitted channels, territory, duration, and restrictions;
- model and property release status;
- consent owner and withdrawal procedure;
- visible brands and approval status;
- original dimensions and checksum;
- approved crops and derivatives;
- alt text;
- owning team, approval date, and next rights-review date.

Do not rely on a source URL alone. Licenses and available pages can change.

Recommended filename pattern:

```text
cgsi-[subject]-[activity]-[location-or-context]-[orientation]-v01.jpg
```

Keep high-resolution masters in the approved digital asset manager or secured media store.
Commit only optimized, approved public derivatives to the web repository when local hosting is
required.

## 11. Accessibility guidance

- Write alt text for the image’s purpose in the page, not as an inventory of visible objects.
- Describe meaningful activity and context: “A broker reviews research notes with a client in a
  meeting room.”
- Do not mention race, age, disability, or emotion unless it is relevant and can be stated
  respectfully from visible evidence.
- Use empty alt text for purely decorative images and avoid duplicating adjacent captions.
- Never embed essential copy, charts, labels, or instructions inside photography.
- Maintain readable contrast for any overlaid text in every responsive crop. Use a solid content
  panel when the image cannot provide reliable contrast.
- Set focal points in the CMS and verify desktop, tablet, mobile, 200% zoom, and high-text-spacing
  crops.
- Avoid automatic carousels. If motion is essential, provide pause controls and respect reduced
  motion.

## 12. Selection and approval workflow

1. Define the communication job and required crop before searching or shooting.
2. Shortlist by authenticity and subject relevance, not only visual attractiveness.
3. Verify source, license, releases, consent, trademarks, location, and confidentiality.
4. Review the image with the page copy so it does not imply an unsupported service or
   relationship.
5. Create and inspect responsive crops.
6. Add alt text, rights metadata, owner, and next-review date.
7. Obtain design, content-owner, legal/compliance, and privacy approval where applicable.
8. Publish only the approved derivative.
9. Recheck rights during material reuse, campaign changes, rebranding, or scheduled review.

The final test is simple: the photograph should look as though a careful documentary photographer
understood CGSI’s work and the people it serves. If it looks chosen because “finance websites need
an image here,” it should not be used.
