# CGSI content and copy guide

## Voice

Write as a capable firm speaking clearly to a serious person. The voice is:

- Calm rather than promotional.
- Specific rather than vague.
- Professional without legalistic clutter.
- Direct without sounding transactional.
- Human through accountability, not slang or forced warmth.

Prefer verbs such as `clarify`, `coordinate`, `review`, `understand`, `confirm`, `support`, and
`maintain`. Explain what CGSI does around a decision instead of describing the firm with empty
adjectives.

## Message hierarchy

Most pages should answer these questions in sequence:

1. What is this page or service?
2. Who is it for?
3. What does CGSI actually do?
4. What does the process involve?
5. What information, risk, eligibility, or limitation matters?
6. What is the next appropriate action?

The headline earns attention. The supporting text makes the scope concrete. The body explains the
relationship or process. The CTA should be a useful next step, not a pressure tactic.

## Homepage language

The current hero direction is `A partner beyond the trade.` The supporting copy describes daily
market insight, broker-assisted trading, post-trade support, clarifying decisions, coordinating
execution, and staying informed after the trade.

The current homepage services heading and supporting paragraph are intentionally stable while the
category selector changes. Do not rewrite them merely to make the tabs work.

The About section uses `Institutional capability. Human accountability.` This pairing is useful
because it holds the firm's institutional posture and human connection together without claiming a
testimonial or outcome.

## Service language

Use `Our services` for the public section label. `Services` is clearer than `Capabilities` when the
section is describing what a brokerage offers to clients. `Solutions` may be used only when the
content describes a defined structure for a particular need and an approved owner confirms that
term.

The homepage groups current services into these working families:

- Market access.
- Research and intelligence.
- Investment solutions.
- Operations and support.

These are presentation categories, not necessarily legal or operational product classifications.
They can change when approved service taxonomy becomes available.

## Audience language

Speak to the situation, responsibility, and need around an audience. Avoid implying that a single
service is appropriate for everyone. Current audience pathways are:

- Individuals and families.
- Institutions and corporations.
- OFWs and seafarers.
- New investors.

High-net-worth individuals and international businesses are important context from the project
brief, but do not add a new public audience page or claim a dedicated offering without approved
content.

## Claims and compliance boundaries

Do not write or imply:

- Guaranteed returns, profit, safety, or certainty.
- Risk-free investing or protection from market loss.
- A fiduciary, advisory, discretionary, or managed relationship unless the exact legal and
  operational status is approved.
- A product being available to every visitor.
- A license, regulatory status, market access, fee, tax benefit, or eligibility condition that is
  not sourced and current.
- Client counts, assets under management, market share, years of experience, or testimonial claims
  that are not approved.
- That research is personalized advice or a recommendation.

Use risk-aware language such as `may`, `can involve`, `subject to eligibility`, `review current
rules`, and `does not guarantee an outcome` where appropriate. Every service page needs a reviewed
risk and disclosure treatment.

## CTA guidance

Primary actions:

- `Open an account`
- `Speak with CGSI`

Useful secondary actions:

- `Market insights`
- `Our company`
- `Governance & oversight`
- `Review requirements`
- `Explore services`

Do not place multiple bright primary buttons in the same region. A low-priority action should not
compete visually with account opening.

## Leadership content

The leadership section should show real accountability: name, approved role, a dedicated portrait
slot, and a concise responsibility or biography. The current local data has names and roles only.
The homepage uses role-based responsibility summaries and intentionally blank portrait slots until
approved images are added. Do not invent credentials, employment history, qualifications, or
portraits.

When approved biographies arrive, store them in the People CMS collection or the agreed typed
content source, include review ownership, and add an approved portrait with rights and alt text.

## Research and market content

Market values need an `as of` time, source label, source link, and a clear state such as market
open or closed. Publications need author or source, publication date, review date where relevant,
and the right disclosure context. Do not make market movement sound like a call to action.

## Editing checklist

- Is the subject and audience clear in the first two lines?
- Does the copy describe a real CGSI action or process?
- Is every factual claim sourced and current?
- Does the copy avoid product-first jargon for visitors who are still orienting themselves?
- Does the CTA match the visitor's stage?
- Could a compliance reviewer identify the scope and limitations?
- Does the page remain understandable without its image or visual styling?
